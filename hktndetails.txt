The challenge
Build an AI dark factory
You build a software factory in BAND Desktop: a band of coding agents that plans work, implements it, hands off evidence, and independently checks its own results. You give it a job and decide whether to accept what comes back. What you submit is the factory, the run that produced the result, and the result.

That is the promise of an AI dark factory — a software production process that keeps working without a person directing every step. Making the collaboration reliable is an engineering problem in its own right, and this hackathon is a concrete way to attack it. You design the team, its handoffs and its checks, then see whether it can build a service, repair failures, and extend what it built without breaking what already works.

Two tracks
Both are clean-room clones of a product everyone knows. Pick one and stay in it — you compete only against the track you chose.

🍽️ tablekeeper

A restaurant reservation system, like OpenTable

The hard part: a table must never be double-booked, under concurrency, retries and time zones.

💸 pocketful

A wallet and payments app, like Venmo

The hard part: money must never be created, destroyed or spent twice, under concurrent transfers, retries and rounding.

Before you start building
How it works
How to participate
The hackathon takes place online on the lablab.ai platform and the lablab.ai Discord server. Please register for both in order to participate. To join, click the Enroll button at the bottom of the page and read the Hackathon Guidelines, the Getting Started Guide and the lablab.ai Hackathon Rule Book.

The hackathon is fully online and open to everyone, anywhere in the world.

If you need help with Band Desktop, you can ask the Band team in the Band discord server

Teams
Teams consist of 1-6 people. If you don't have a team, don't worry. You can connect with other participants from all over the world on the dashboard or on the Discord server, where you can also find teammates and bounce around ideas.

Submission requirements
Everything your entry needs, in one list.

Your band
At least three distinct coding-agent seats in BAND Desktop, each with a mandate file. Seats may share a runtime and a model.
A seat can be any runtime BAND supports, including one you build on the BAND SDK and run on your own machine, a cloud box or a CI runner.
Models: use your Featherless credits or bring your own model-provider access.
Your mandates must be generic This is important
A mandate is the standing instruction you give a seat (agent). It says how that seat works: what it owns, how it takes and hands off work, when it rejects something. It must be generic. It cannot name anything specific to your track or the challenge: no endpoint paths, no field names, no error codes. The track's detail belongs in the task you paste into the room, not in a seat's standing instructions.

The test: could you hand your mandates to a team building something completely different, and would they still make sense? If not, you have written a transcript of this problem rather than a factory.

A mandate naming track-specific detail disqualifies the entry.

Your repository
One public GitHub repository a judge can clone without BAND Desktop membership.
One folder per stage you completed — stage-1/ through stage-4/ — each a complete, buildable service. Submit only the stages you completed. Minimum to be eligible: a complete stage 1.
Plus: your seat mandates, your factory description, and the export of the BAND Desktop room your band worked in .
Your video
Your video must include a recording of the BAND Desktop room that generated your solution, and a walkthrough.
A video without the room recording disqualifies your team.
Your service
It must build and serve from a clean container with no outbound network. Test that before you submit — a service that doesn't start scores zero.
CPU and memory caps, harness concurrency and per-request timeouts are published with the spec at kickoff.
Disqualifiers at a glance
A mandate that names track-specific detail
A video without the BAND Desktop room recording
A service that does not start from a clean container

Judging criteria
50%
Factory
Generic, effective and reusable: mandates another team could point at a different problem, how far through the four stages it got with code that meets the spec, and a FACTORY.md that is enough to stand it up — seat setup, design rationale, measured costs, and how it catches and recovers from bad work

25%
App
What the factory built: a coherent, presentation-ready, responsive UI over maintainable code

25%
Agent Teamwork
The seats did the work together, and without you.

Collaboration: the seats really shared the work — review changed something, handoffs carried the whole task, and the code traces to the room.

Autonomy: in the run you submit, the task you dispatch for each stage is the only human input — no steering, approvals or reruns
